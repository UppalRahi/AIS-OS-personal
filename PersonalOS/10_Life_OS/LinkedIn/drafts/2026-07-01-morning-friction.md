## Post body

I used to think outbound was a volume problem.

More leads. More sends. More tools.

A year inside enrichment pipelines and send schedules changed that.

The friction I keep running into is smaller than that.

It's the same 40 minutes every morning.

Did the webhook fire.
Is this list actually clean.
Did that reply land in the right place.

None of it is hard.
All of it repeats.

And it sits right before the work that needs your head — prioritising accounts, reading a reply properly, deciding what to do next.

I've started calling this "pre-work."

The copy-paste layer that doesn't show up in any job description but quietly owns your calendar.

My instinct as an engineer was to automate everything.

That made things worse for a while.

What helped was narrower: find the step you already did fifty times this week. Remove that one first.

Not a platform swap.
Not a six-month project.
Just one loop you stop running by hand.

I'm still learning how to write about this without it sounding like a pitch.

Probably because I'm an introvert who thinks in systems and only later finds the words.

If you work in GTM or ops — what's the small thing that eats your morning before the real work starts?
